Measuring runoff in the Mohave Mountains: an open validation protocol

The estimate tool on the main page relies on an assumed runoff coefficient (0.20–0.60) and on valley-floor rainfall. This protocol replaces those assumptions with measurements anyone can repeat and check.

It answers two open questions: how much rain falls on the mountain, and what fraction runs off (open questions). Citations refer to the numbered references on the main page.

Status: No measurements have been made yet. This page is a method, not a set of results.

1. Rain gauge placement

Rationale. WMO's recommended minimum density for mountainous regions is one non-recording gauge per 250 km²82. That is meant for regional networks. The Falls Springs Wash catchment is only about 13 km², and monsoon cells are small, so this protocol uses a much denser design to resolve how rainfall changes with elevation (a design choice, not a WMO requirement).

  • Service the gauges after every storm and at least monthly.

2. Measuring flow in a small wash

Pick a short reach of bedrock or stable channel near the D1 outlet. Fix a thin-plate weir across it, sealed to the bedrock so water cannot bypass the plate. Install a stage logger or staff gauge in the pool upstream.

Option A: 90° V-notch (best for small flows)

Q = 2.49 · h2.48    (Q in ft³/s, h = head in ft)

This is the Cone equation for a fully contracted standard 90° V-notch. Use it only for 0.05–4.25 ft³/s. Keep the notch at least two heads from the channel sides and bottom. Measure head at least four heads upstream81.

Option B: about 1 m (3.28 ft) sharp-crested rectangular weir

Q = 3.33 · h3/2 · (L − 0.2h)    (Q in ft³/s; L, h in ft)

This is the Francis equation for a standard fully contracted rectangular weir. Do not use it for heads greater than one-third of the crest length. Crest height and side contractions must be at least 2 × the maximum head. Measure head at least 4 × the maximum head upstream56. If these conditions can't be met, rate the weir with the Kindsvater–Carter method56.

Option C: bucket-and-timer (very small flows)

Q (gal/s) = bucket volume (gal) ÷ fill time (s)

Direct the whole flow through a pipe or spout into a bucket of known volume. Repeat at least three times and average.

Runoff volume

Integrate discharge over the hydrograph. Sum Q × Δt across the logger intervals and convert: 1 ft³ = 7.48052 gal.

3. Flash-flood safety

  • Never be in the wash during a storm. Install, read and service equipment only in dry weather. Retrieve logger data afterwards.
  • Flash floods can arrive from distant storms over the mountains. Check forecasts and warnings, and leave washes immediately if a Flash Flood Warning is issued83.
  • Six inches of fast-moving water can knock an adult down84,85. Do not drive or park in washes; “Turn Around Don't Drown”84.
  • Debris flows can start on dry slopes after only minutes of intense rain85. Abandoned mine shafts line the access road; stay out of them38.
  • Work in pairs, carry water, and avoid summer midday heat. Leave a trip plan with someone.

4. Logging template

Download runoff_log_template.csv (CC0). Use one row per gauge or stage reading per event. Fields include date; storm start, end and duration; rainfall depth per gauge; stage readings and discharge; bucket volume and time; runoff volume; catchment area; antecedent soil moisture (dry, damp or wet, or a sensor value); observer; photo references; and notes. Leave fields blank rather than estimating.

5. Computing the runoff coefficient

C = runoff volume ÷ (rainfall depth × catchment area)

Use consistent units. For example, runoff in gallons ÷ (rainfall in inches × area in acres × 27,154 gal per acre-inch). Use the area-weighted rainfall from the gauges inside the catchment. Report C for each event, along with rainfall depth, rainfall intensity and antecedent moisture: C varies from storm to storm.

How results update the estimate tool: The tool's coefficient range is currently assumed (0.20–0.60). Once at least five storm events have been measured at the D1 weir, the low and high bounds will be replaced by the 10th and 90th percentile measured event coefficients, and the mid value by the median. Each change will be committed with the data in the repository, so it can be audited.

6. Permission notes

  • Most of the Mohave Mountains are public land administered by the BLM Lake Havasu Field Office. Contact the office before placing any gauge, weir or logger, and ask what authorization is needed51.
  • Federal authorizations may trigger NEPA review52 and Section 106 cultural-resources consultation55.
  • Stay clear of the radio-tower compound; warning signs are posted there38.
  • Drone flights follow FAA Part 10747. See the roadmap.
